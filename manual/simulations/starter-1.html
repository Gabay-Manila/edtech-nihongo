Theme: Leaving Work Greetings, Food Invitations, Daily Workplace Routines

1. Overview

This case study reproduces the standard 45-minute lesson flow of the EdTech Nihongo Instructor Manual for Starter Set 1, dramatized as a dialogue between a "Teacher AI" and a "Student AI." It walks through the set's key (★) teaching cards in the order a real class would meet them: a quick rapid-drill pass, followed by a focused roleplay on the item the student found hardest.

2. Full Simulation Dialogue
Turn Speaker Utterance / Dialogue Content Manual Reference
1 Teacher AI Hello! Let's begin today's lesson. Today's theme is: Leaving Work Greetings, Food Invitations, Daily Workplace Routines. First, let's do a quick sound warm-up together, then move into the app questions. Pre-teach (00–05 min)
2 Teacher AI You finished work. Greet your team before leaving. Q1 Selected Card – Instructor Script
3 Student AI “Osaki ni shitsurei shimasu!”
4 Teacher AI Great! Let's keep the pace. Next: Invite a colleague to eat together at the cafeteria. Q2 Selected Card – Instructor Script
5 Student AI “...” (hesitates, unsure how to complete the sentence)
6 Teacher AI That's okay, take a moment. Remember the point: Place + "de" + "isshoni" + "~masen ka?". Practice responding "Ii desu ne, ikimashou!" Phase 1 – Note incorrect Q numbers
7 Student AI “Shokudou de isshoni tabemasen ka?!” (self-correction, correct)
8 Teacher AI Good, I'll note that one for later practice. (Continues through Q5, Q8, Q11 at a steady pace — the student answers all three correctly with growing confidence.) Q5, Q8, Q11 Selected Cards – Q1–Q15 Full Index
9 Teacher AI Good work, that's the end of the first half! Let's go back to “Inviting to Lunch” and drill it thoroughly out loud. Phase 2: Roleplay & Drill (25–40 min)
10 Teacher AI Once more: Invite a colleague to eat together at the cafeteria. Say it clearly this time. Q2 Explanation & Foundation Reinforcement
11 Student AI “Shokudou de isshoni tabemasen ka?!” (immediate, confident response)
12 Teacher AI Perfect. One more before we close: You are holding heavy boxes. Ask a nearby colleague for a hand. Q13 Selected Card – Instructor Script
13 Student AI “Chotto tetsudatte kudasai!”
14 Teacher AI Excellent, well done today. For homework, please review Q2 and Q13 in the app, five times each. Otsukaresama deshita — mata ashita! Wrap-up (40–45 min) / Feedback & Homework Setting
3. Review Points
3-1. Time Management Framework

The four-phase structure — Pre-teach → Rapid Drill → Roleplay & Drill → Wrap-up — is confirmed across the turns above. The student's hesitation on Q2 is logged rather than corrected on the spot, and only revisited once the full rapid drill is complete, exactly as the manual's teaching policy specifies.

3-2. Response to Errors

Rather than supplying the answer immediately, the Teacher AI restates the underlying grammar point — Place + "de" + "isshoni" + "~masen ka?". Practice responding "Ii desu ne, ikimashou!" — and gives the student room to self-correct. This "hint by principle" approach lets the student rebuild the sentence themselves, which the manual treats as more durable than direct correction.

3-3. Assessing Learner Level

The student answers straightforward items fluently but needs a beat and a grammar reminder for the more complex pattern in Q2. This profile matches the persona's level on the Learner Level Matrix, and the chunked, repeat-until-confident drill in Phase 2 is the correct next action for that level.

4. Training Discussion Questions
Q1. Why does the Teacher AI restate the grammar point for Q2 instead of simply giving the correct sentence?
Q2. If the student had also hesitated on Q13, how should the Phase 2 focus change?
Q3. How would you adapt this same set of cards for a Level 1 (Zero/Slow) student instead?
Q4. Adding "Chotto" softens requests in Japanese workplace culture. What real workplace situation, beyond the one in this set, could reuse this same grammar point?
